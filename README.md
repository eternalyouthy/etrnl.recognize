# Recognize

**Learn languages by finding connections, not just memorizing words.**

## Learning a language doesn't mean starting from scratch

When I started learning German, I found it pretty difficult. I treated it as something completely new: memorizing words, learning grammar rules, and trying to put everything together.

Over time, I realized that this approach wasn't really working for me. **A new language isn't completely separate from the languages we already know.**

I started comparing words, looking at their roots, noticing similarities in how sentences are built, and sometimes exploring etymology. Suddenly, a lot of things started making more sense.

What I found especially interesting was how **people speaking different languages often describe the world in similar ways.**

For example, take the English *footprint* and the German *Fußabdruck*. Both describe the mark a foot leaves behind, and their parts have similar meanings:

- **foot + print** — foot + impression
- **Fuß + Abdruck** — foot + impression

There are plenty of examples like this. Sometimes, understanding how a word is built makes it much easier to remember.

This changed the way I approach language learning, and it's the main idea behind ETRNL.

## What is ETRNL?

**ETRNL Recognize** is a free, open-source project for learning languages through comparisons, patterns, and practice.

It currently supports three languages: **Russian, English, and German**.

You can choose which language you already know and which one you want to learn. For example, you can learn German through Russian, English through German, or Russian through English.

I also recommend trying the reverse direction. If you're learning German through Russian, try switching to Russian through German. You'll be reading German explanations of words you already understand.

It's a way to practice your German without having to learn new concepts at the same time.

## What's inside?

The project includes vocabulary, grammar, word roots, expressions, and practice exercises.

One thing I focus on is learning **ready-made expressions**, often called *chunks*.

We don't build every sentence from scratch, even in our native language. We use familiar phrases and patterns all the time.

For example:

*“I think it's important because...”*

You already have most of the sentence ready. You just need to finish your thought.

That's why the **Expressions** section focuses on useful phrases, idioms, and sentence patterns rather than just individual words.

In **Roots**, you can explore word roots, prefixes, and how different words are connected.

There's already plenty of content to explore, learn something new, and see languages from a different perspective. I'll keep adding more over time.

## Why did I make this?

I'm not a linguist, and I don't think there's only one correct way to learn a language.

I simply found an approach that worked better for me and wanted to build a tool around it.

I started ETRNL mainly for myself, but decided to make it available to anyone who might find it useful.

**Compare. Recognize. Understand.**

*Don't just memorize words. Try to understand how they work.*
